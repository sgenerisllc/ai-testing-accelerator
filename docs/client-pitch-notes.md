# 🎯 Client Pitch Notes

Internal notes for the sales/business development team. Use these to tailor pitches by audience and overcome common objections.

---

## The Core Narrative (90 seconds)

> "Your QA team is doing 2018 work in 2026. They're hand-writing tests, spending hours triaging flakes, and losing knowledge every time someone leaves.
>
> We turn them into 2026 engineers: AI-augmented, agent-driven, autonomous. In 8 weeks. For $500 per person.
>
> The math: $3,000 investment → ~$400K saved in year one. Want to see five demos that prove it?"

---

## Audience-Specific Angles

### CTO / VP Engineering

**Lead with:** Speed-to-market, hiring leverage, competitive advantage.

**Hot buttons:**
- "Engineering velocity is your #1 KPI. Flaky tests are the silent killer."
- "Hiring a new AI-savvy QA engineer costs $200K+. Upskilling existing team: $500/head."
- "Companies adopting AI-augmented QA now will have a 2-year lead by 2027."

**Avoid:** Implementation minutiae. Send them to the executive proposal.

---

### QA Director / Manager

**Lead with:** Team morale, career growth, day-to-day quality of life.

**Hot buttons:**
- "Your team is doing the same toil they were doing 5 years ago. They know it. AI offers them growth."
- "Capstone projects turn into real internal tools — your team ships something they're proud of."
- "Certification on resume = retention. Engineers stay where they grow."

**Avoid:** Talking about "replacing QA jobs." This is augmentation, not replacement.

---

### Head of DevOps / Platform

**Lead with:** CI reliability, deployment frequency, cost controls.

**Hot buttons:**
- "Self-healing tests reduce CI flake rate to under 2%."
- "AI failure analyzer cuts on-call burden by 80%."
- "Our patterns work with your stack — GitHub Actions, GitLab CI, Jenkins, all of it."

**Avoid:** UI/E2E specifics. They care about pipelines.

---

### Hands-on Engineering Lead

**Lead with:** The demos. Just show them.

**Hot buttons:**
- Run Demo 05 live. The autonomous agent loop sells itself.
- Show the GitHub repo. Tech leads trust code over slides.
- Offer them admin access during the program.

**Avoid:** Marketing language. Be technical, specific, and let the demos speak.

## Pricing Conversations

### "Is $500 negotiable?"

For 6-person cohorts: hold the price. The math works at this number.

For 12+ participants: discount to $400/head.

For enterprise (3+ cohorts/year): custom quote, typically $300/head + retainer.

### "Why $500 — what's it cost you to deliver?"

Don't disclose internal costs. Reframe:

> "Pricing reflects the value, not our cost. Our customers see ~130× ROI in year one. We're priced to be obvious-yes for any team feeling QA pain."

### "Can we pilot with 2-3 people first?"

Yes — but with a minimum of $1,500 (3 × $500). Below 3 participants, the cohort dynamic suffers.

Better pitch: "Pick your 3 most curious engineers. They become internal champions and train the rest of the team after."

## Objection Handling

### "We already have Copilot — isn't this redundant?"

> "Copilot makes individuals faster at writing code. This accelerator builds team-level systems: autonomous agents, self-healing tests, RAG knowledge bases. Different problem, different solution. The two are complementary."

### "We can't move our data to OpenAI."

> "Neither can our healthcare and fintech customers. Every demo runs offline. For production, we teach local-model patterns: Llama 3 via Ollama, sentence-transformers, etc. No data leaves your environment."

### "Our team is too junior."

> "Half our participants are mid-level QA with 2-3 years experience. The curriculum starts with Python and Playwright fundamentals. By Week 4 even juniors are writing AI-augmented tests. By Week 8 they ship autonomous agents."

### "We don't have time for an 8-week program."

> "It's 6 hours of live session per week — same as one good engineering all-hands. The labs are self-paced. And the program pays for itself in saved engineering hours by Week 4."

### "Can we just buy your demo code and figure it out ourselves?"

> "The code is MIT-licensed and free. But the value of the program is the *transformation* — your team going from passive consumers to confident builders. Try the demos, then decide if you want guided depth."

(Hand them the repo URL. Honest answer wins trust.)

### "We need to think about it."

Always offer the **free demo session** as a low-friction next step:

> "Totally fair. Want me to run a 45-minute live demo for your full team, no commitment? Often that helps the conversation."

## Closing Moves

### The Soft Close

> "Based on what you've shared, this seems like a fit. The next step is a 30-minute discovery call. Tuesday or Thursday work better?"

### The Time-Pressure Close

> "We have one more cohort spot opening next month, then nothing until Q3. Want me to hold the spot while you confirm internally?"

### The Pilot Close

> "If you're not 100% sure, let's start with one cohort of 6. If your team isn't transformed by Week 8, we'll discount the next cohort by 50%. That's our standard win-win."

### The CFO Close

> "Forward this one-pager to your CFO: [link]. The ROI math is on page 1. If they say no, I'll buy you lunch."

## Don't Pitch These Things

- ❌ Don't promise specific tooling outcomes — every team's stack is different
- ❌ Don't compare directly to specific competitors by name (Cypress AI, Mabl, etc.)
- ❌ Don't claim "no flaky tests ever" — claim "60% reduction"
- ❌ Don't guarantee job promotions — say "graduates have moved into roles like..."
- ❌ Don't quote AI capabilities that aren't in the demos

Honesty + demos > inflated marketing.

## Logistics Checklist for First Contact

- [ ] Send one-page summary within 1 business day
- [ ] Reference at least one of their public products in the email
- [ ] Offer 3 specific demo time slots
- [ ] CC the relevant TL or engineering manager (not just the CTO)
- [ ] Follow up at +3 days if no response
- [ ] Mark as cold after 14 days
